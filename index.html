SHADI ZAQTAN

Songwriter & Composer

ABOUT

For the past fifteen years, I've worked — perhaps without much of a plan — to create a new branch of Palestinian song: contemporary in its sound, modern in its language, yet rooted deeply in Palestinian human and political reality.

I start from the idea that a song isn't just entertainment, but a tool for understanding reality and shaping it. So I don't just revisit the past — I try to build a different present. In my music, the guitar — through the one playing it — escapes the classical balcony and lands in the middle of the street.

I blend multiple influences: blues, Latin American protest song, the tradition of Sheikh Imam, alongside my family's musical inheritance — to offer a musical language that places Palestinian song in a global context without losing its identity.

In the Palestinian condition, art becomes a basic need, not a luxury. I don't have final answers about the relationship between song and return, but I believe that telling the story, keeping the memory, and making a free voice heard are all forms of resistance. In that sense, a song can be one possible path home.

Alongside my music, I've tried to build independent artistic spaces free from funding conditions and imposed frameworks — I founded and ran cultural spaces like "Bar Al-Maghara" in Ramallah, an open home for free experimentation and live performance, a platform for artists outside commercial and institutional constraints.

Moving instruments across borders was never easy — on the road to Palestine, and then out of it. Living inside and outside the Palestinian reality enriched that journey; it produced five albums along the way, and I'm currently working on several projects across a few cities, where my stories cross paths with others' — others fleeing a homeland, any homeland, or fleeing toward one.

Poetry runs in the family — his grandfather Khalil Zaqtan was a classical poet, and his father Ghassan Zaqtan is one of the leading voices in modern Arabic poetry.

Aghani Al Madina 2018

Story

Grew out of "The Ramallah Sessions" — a project by Belgian musician Thomas Noël ("The City's Song"), who traveled to Ramallah in 2016 and again in 2017 with violinist Wouter Vandenabeele and photographer Sammy Van Cauteren to record with local musicians. Released as the album The Ramallah Sessions in October 2018.

Featured Track — "Dawleh" ("A State")

Written and composed by Shadi Zaqtan, arranged by Thomas Noël. Performed by Shadi Zaqtan (vocals), Thomas Noël (piano), Wouter Vandenabeele (violin), Ahmed Hawwash (clarinet), François Taillefer (percussion), and Lara Rosseel (double bass).

People Involved

Story

JERIES BABISH

Frequent musical partner. Jeries Babish is a Palestinian singer, songwriter, and producer — vocalist, saxophone, and guitar player in the indie-rock band Mafar, and a regular presence at Studio Al-Mukhtabar. He's one of Shadi's closest musical collaborators, showing up across several projects in very different roles: arranger, saxophonist, co-writer, and duet partner.

Circus

Jeries played bass and saxophone and handled the arrangement — pulling the sound away from the guitar-and-voice format of the first two albums toward a fuller ensemble.

Paranoia

A separate, more experimental mini-album made together at Studio Al-Mukhtabar — a joint collection outside the "Circus" project, built around a looser, freer sound.

New Zealand — Māori / Palestinian

Same Sky

Written with Māori master carver and musician Arekatera "Katz" Maihi in Ramallah in 2016, during a visit by a group of ten Māori artists to collaborate with Palestinian musicians. Left unfinished, it was completed years later and released March 15, 2020 as a koha (gift) to the 51 victims of the Christchurch mosque shootings — built around the idea that everyone lives under the same sky.

Italy — Cultural Solidarity

Perditempo "Libri, vini e vinili"

One of Naples' old cultural landmarks — a bar, bookstore, and record store on Via San Pietro a Maiella, run for years by owner Luca. In spirit, it's a lot like Bar Al-Maghara in Ramallah. When Al-Maghara had to close, Perditempo held several solidarity events, put up posters of Palestinian artists across Naples, and took part in benefit events for the cause. Shadi has performed there three times, including at Al-Maghara Day, held at Perditempo itself.

Location
Belgium / Palestine — "The City's Song"

The Ramallah Sessions

In April 2016, Belgian musician Thomas Noël — initiator of "The City's Song" — arrived in Ramallah with violinist Wouter Vandenabeele and photographer Sammy Van Cauteren, later joined by Faris Shomali (Bel3ax) and oud player Noor Alraee. A second visit followed in May 2017. Released as The Ramallah Sessions in October 2018, featuring "Dawleh (A State)," "Ta'akher El Bahar," "O Fi Balad," "Taftish," and "Khyoul."
